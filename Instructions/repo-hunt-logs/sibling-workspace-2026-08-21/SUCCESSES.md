## LOC forecast correction

Early reference size is a weak scope forecast, not an outcome label:

- Railway's rejected immediate preview was 81 strict effective additions; the
  accepted reference reached 638, and a representative legitimate solver added
  more than 800 production lines across six files.
- Calamine's 128-line Level 1 reference produced a 284-line successful-solver
  median.
- PcapPlusPlus's early 158-effective-line reference produced three legitimate
  predecessor solutions at about 267, 420, and 525 effective additions.

These successes do not make LOC irrelevant. They show that preliminary LOC
must have low rating influence and explicit uncertainty. Actual successful
solver medians replace it. Repeated independent convergence remains strong
negative evidence: str0m's ten solvers stayed in one file and 97-147 production
logic lines, so that observed batch—not the initial estimate—closed its scope.

## Positive selection patterns

The accepted problems share useful properties:

- the operation is native to the repository rather than a generic algorithm;
- correctness crosses several existing subsystems or execution modes;
- public behavior supplies deterministic black-box or round-trip oracles;
- plausible shortcuts are derived from real solver trajectories and mutation
  survivors rather than imagined private implementations;
- the reference is honest and unpadded, while successful solver size is measured
  independently; and
- acceptance leaves a compact live problem record plus recoverable cold
  evidence under `archive/`.

Success does not erase similarity risk. An accepted task shape becomes stronger
local prior art and should make neighboring tasks harder to justify, not easier.
