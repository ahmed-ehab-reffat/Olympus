# Add modal analysis and modal response to sfepy

SfePy assembles stiffness and mass matrices but leaves the vibration engineering to scripts. Add `sfepy.discrete.modal`, exporting `assemble_matrices`, `solve_modes`, `ModalSolution`, `rayleigh_coefficients` and `rayleigh_damping_ratios` through `sfepy.discrete`, and give `Problem` a `solve_modes` taking the same arguments.

`assemble_matrices(problem, stiffness='stiffness', mass='mass')` assembles the two named equations into sparse matrices in the reduced space the problem's conditions give, the linear combination operator applied on both sides. `solve_modes(problem, n_modes=6, stiffness='stiffness', mass='mass')` returns the `n_modes` lowest solutions of `K phi = lambda M phi`, ascending, as a `ModalSolution`. It, not the assembly, needs one unknown variable whose field has one component per dimension.

A `ModalSolution` exposes `eigenvalues`, `omegas` the square root of each, `frequencies` in hertz, the reduced and full mode shapes as `reduced_vectors` and `vectors`, a column per mode, the assembled `mtx_k` and `mtx_m`, and `n_modes`. A mode vector spans the full space, is zero where an essential condition fixes it, and passes back through the linear combination operator. Modes arrive scaled to unit modal mass, and whatever the scaling, the largest magnitude component of each full vector is positive, ties to the lowest index. `modal_masses()` and `modal_stiffnesses()` give `phi^T M phi` and `phi^T K phi`. `normalize(kind)` returns a rescaled copy, leaving the original and the frequencies alone: `mass` and `stiffness` make the matching modal quantity one, `max` the largest magnitude of the full vector, `norm` its Euclidean length.

`expand` lifts a reduced vector, `restrict` takes a full one at the free degrees of freedom, `project` gives the modal coordinates `phi^T M x` over the modal mass. `rigid_body_vectors(centre=None)` spans the full space, translations then rotations, three columns in two dimensions and six in three, centred on the origin by default. Positions are the field nodes, which outnumber the mesh vertices above first order. A rotation column is its axis crossed with the position measured from the centre, in two dimensions minus the height and the width.

`participation_factors(centre=None)` is `phi_i^T M r_j` in the reduced space, the rigid columns restricted the same way, a row per mode. `effective_masses` divides each squared factor by that mode's modal mass, so rescaling the modes does not move it, `mass_fractions` divides those by `r_j^T M r_j`, and `cumulative_mass_fractions` accumulates down the modes.

`mac(other)` is the squared inner product of every full mode vector with every one of the other, over the product of their own. `pair(other)` returns two arrays holding one entry per mode: the index of the other mode of the largest such value, and that value. `select(fmin=None, fmax=None, n_modes=None)` keeps the modes inside the closed band, then the first `n_modes` of them, and may keep none. A solution of no modes still answers all of these: per-mode arrays with no rows, an empty `create_output`, every modal response zero, and a static correction that is the whole static response.

`frequency_response(freqs, load, damping=0.0, correction='none')` sums `phi_i (phi_i^T f)` over `k_i - w^2 m_i + 2j zeta_i w w_i m_i` for `w = 2 pi f`, a complex row per frequency in hertz. Any damping argument takes either a single ratio or one per mode. `static_correction(load)` is the exact static response to that load less what the modes carry of it; `correction` set to `static` adds the whole of it at every frequency. `base_frequency_response(freqs, direction, damping=0.0, centre=None)` uses that same modal denominator with the modal load set to minus the participation factor, giving displacement relative to the base under a unit base acceleration along one rigid body direction.

`response_spectrum(spectrum, direction, damping=0.0, rule='srss', centre=None)` takes one spectral acceleration per mode and scales each mode by its participation factor in that direction times that value, over the modal mass times the squared angular frequency. `srss` is the square root of the summed squares, `abssum` the summed magnitudes, and `cqc` the square root of the double sum weighted by `8 zeta^2 (1 + r) r^1.5` over `(1 - r^2)^2 + 4 zeta^2 r (1 + r)^2` for `r` the ratio of the two angular frequencies, which needs one ratio shared by every mode.

`transient_response(times, load, history, damping=0.0, displacement=None, velocity=None)` scales the load in time by `history`, a real row per sample. Samples are equally spaced and increasing, at least two, one history value each; only their spacing counts, so the record may start anywhere. The load varies linearly between consecutive samples and each modal equation is solved exactly for it, from the initial displacement and velocity projected onto the modes, or from rest.

`create_output(name='mode')` returns the mode shapes keyed `mode000` upwards, each the output data `Problem` saves for it, a row per mesh vertex and a column per component. `rayleigh_coefficients(f1, f2, zeta1, zeta2)` gives the mass and stiffness proportional coefficients matching two damping ratios at two frequencies, and `rayleigh_damping_ratios(alpha, beta, frequencies)` reads them back as half of `alpha` over the angular frequency plus `beta` times it, in any order.

A load reduces through the transpose of the linear combination operator, so the three load-driven responses hold under such conditions. Everything mapping a full vector the other way raises `ValueError` there instead: `restrict`, `project`, the four participation quantities, `base_frequency_response`, `response_spectrum`, and a transient starting state.

Raise `ValueError` for an equation name none carries, or one name for both; fewer than one mode, or more than the reduced space holds; a normalization, correction or rule not named above; a wrongly sized vector wherever one is taken; a centre of the wrong length; `mac` between unlike degree-of-freedom counts, or `pair` whose other solution holds no mode; a reversed band or a count below one.

The dynamic methods add a negative frequency or ratio, a damping neither one ratio nor one per mode, a transient ratio at or above one, a spectrum of the wrong length, a direction outside the rigid body columns, a `cqc` damping not shared by every mode, fewer than two samples, samples unevenly spaced or not increasing, and a wrongly sized history. `rayleigh_coefficients` wants two frequencies both positive and in order and no negative ratio, `rayleigh_damping_ratios` a positive frequency.
