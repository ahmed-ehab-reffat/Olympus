# Add specular reflection paths to acoustic propagation

Acoular propagates sound straight from source to microphone. Add reflecting panels so it also arrives after bouncing off them.

Both new classes live in `acoular.environments`. `ReflectingPlane` is a flat rectangle from a `corner` with two edges `edge1`, `edge2`, perpendicular and non-zero; anything else raises a `ValueError`. Its read only `normal` is the unit vector along their cross product. `signed_distance` measures distance along that normal, `mirror` reflects positions, `mirror_direction` reflects directions and ignores where the plane sits, and `contains` reports points on the plane inside the rectangle, corners and edges included, judging on-plane by an absolute distance, not one growing with the coordinates. An edge replaced later is checked the same way. Use 3xN coordinate arrays throughout, returning one value per point or another 3xN array; anything else raises a `ValueError`, as does a `crossing` whose sides differ in shape.

`crossing(start, end)` takes two such arrays of segment ends and returns coordinates and a flag, counting a meeting only strictly between them, so a segment starting or ending on the plane does not cross. Wherever the flag is false, whatever the reason, the coordinates are `nan`. A panel reflects on both faces and is opaque. `factor_at(cosine)` gives a reflection's amplitude factor: `reflection_factor`, default one, at every angle, or `(Z * cosine - 1) / (Z * cosine + 1)` when the normalized `impedance` `Z` is set instead; a non-positive `Z` raises a `ValueError`.

`ReflectiveEnvironment` is an `Environment` with a `planes` list and a `max_order` defaulting to one. `path_sequences()` lists the sequences sound may follow: every tuple of panel indices from length zero up to `max_order` with no two neighbours equal, grouped by increasing length and lexicographic within a group, so the empty tuple comes first. A negative `max_order` raises a `ValueError`. `image(sequence, pos)` mirrors positions about the sequence's panels in the order the ray meets them, emitter to receiver; `image_velocity(sequence, vec)` does the same to directions.

`paths(gpos, mpos)` returns travel distance, amplitude factor and existence for every sequence, in that order, each (P, N, M) for N emitting and M receiving points; `paths_at(gpos, mpos)` gives (P, N) for N matched pairs and rejects unequal counts with a `ValueError`. Travel distance runs from the emitting point's image to the receiver. The amplitude factor is the product of the sequence's panel factors, reported whether or not it exists. Every method taking coordinate arrays accepts an empty batch, keeping the zero-length axis.

Panels in the same plane make the mirroring fold back on itself. An even number of mirrors returns the position unchanged, so that sequence carries the direct travel distance and has no reflection points. An odd number leaves the image where a single mirror would, so it carries the one-bounce distance and keeps only its last contact point, the earlier ones missing. Neither exists as a path, since a missing contact point cannot lie on its own panel.

`reflection_points(index, gpos, mpos)` gives one contact point per panel, shaped (L, 3, N, M) for L panels, N emitters and M receivers. A missing crossing makes that point and every earlier one `nan`, and the amplitude `nan` under an impedance. `incidence(index, gpos, mpos)` drops the coordinate axis and gives the non-negative cosine between the incoming ray and the normal, so either face gives the same, and is `nan` where its point is.

An index outside `path_sequences()`, negative or unknown, raises `IndexError` from `reflection_points` and `incidence`; `image` and `image_velocity` raise `IndexError` for any sequence not in that environment's `path_sequences()`.

A path exists when all its reflection points lie on their own panel and no panel blocks any segment of the folded path. A panel blocks a segment crossing its plane strictly between the endpoints with the crossing inside the rectangle. A panel never blocks a segment meeting it at one of that path's own reflection points, however close that point lands to the plane in floating point. That holds in any orientation, not just panels square to the axes where the arithmetic cancels exactly. The direct path can be blocked too.

`impulse_response(gpos, mpos, sample_freq, num_samples)` samples each pair into (N, M, num_samples): every existing path deposits its amplitude over its travel distance at the delay it needs, split over the two neighbouring samples by the fractional part. Contributions past the last sample are dropped, as is a path of zero length.

`apparent_r` still reports the direct distance. `path_sequences()`, `paths`, `paths_at`, `image`, `image_velocity` and `impulse_response` are on a plain `Environment` too, whose only sequence is empty. `ReflectingPlane` and `ReflectiveEnvironment` each carry a `digest` changing with their traits. `SteeringVector.transfer` adds one term per existing path, the amplitude times the reference distance over the travel distance times `exp(-1j * k * (travel distance - reference distance))`, the reference staying direct.

Every consumer below drops a path that does not exist, separately for each emitting and receiving point, contributing nothing for it even when its amplitude is `nan`. It never shortens what a consumer emits. This holds when the direct path is the only one, so a blocking panel silences the arrival even at `max_order` zero. None of them decide from how many sequences an environment has.

`PointSource`, `PointSourceDipole` and `LineSource` let every monopole radiate along every existing path, delayed by travel distance over the speed of sound and scaled by the amplitude over it. Each still yields blocks of samples by channels, the shape it yielded before, one column per microphone and no extra axis for the paths.

`MovingPointSource` does the same per path, taking each reflected path from the mirrored trajectory: the position mirrors with `image`, the velocity turns with `image_velocity`, which is also the velocity its `conv_amp` amplification uses. Every path's amplitude factor is taken at the emission position belonging to that sample. `BeamformerTime` and `BeamformerTimeSq` add one delayed sample per path and microphone, weighted by the path amplitude and by the steering amplitude their `steer_type` gives for that travel distance against the same direct reference. The autopower removed for `r_diag` uses each microphone's summed contribution.
