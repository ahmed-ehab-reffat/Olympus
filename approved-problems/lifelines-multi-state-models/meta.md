# Add multi state models to the non-parametric fitters

Add an estimator for subjects moving through an arbitrary set of states, not just from alive to dead.

Data is one row per interval a subject occupied a state, in columns `id`, `start`, `stop`, `from_state`, `to_state` and `observed`. A row spans the time after `start` up to and including `stop`, which is also when it puts its subject in the risk set of `from_state`. `observed` is true when the subject moved to `to_state` at `stop`, false when it was merely last seen there, and then `to_state` is ignored. An optional `weights` column holds a positive weight per row, counted wherever a row would otherwise count as one. Rows may leave gaps, and a subject outside its rows is in no risk set.

Raise a `ValueError` for a frame that is empty, holds nulls, lacks a required column, carries a non positive weight, has a stop at or before its start, a negative start, an observed move into the state it leaves, two overlapping intervals of one subject, or an interval opening at the instant an observed move closed another, in a state that move did not enter. An interval may open where a censored one closed.

`MultiStateFitter(alpha=0.05, label=None)` has `fit(transitions, initial_state=None, timeline=None, label=None)`. `states_` lists every state seen as a `from_state` or as an observed move's destination, `transitions_` the ordered pairs some observed move took, `absorbing_states_` those no interval ever begins in, each ascending. The transition times are the distinct stops of observed rows; `timeline` is zero then those, unless the caller supplies its own, which is rejected the same way unless it is strictly increasing and non negative.

At each transition time a pair's intensity increment is the weight moving along it over the weight at risk in the state it leaves, a state with nothing at risk contributing nothing, and each row of the increment matrix sums to zero. The probability of moving between two times is the product of identity plus increment over the transition times after the first and up to the second, in increasing time order; `transition_matrix(t, since=0.0, markov=True)` reports it as a frame whose rows are the state held at `since`. `initial_distribution_` is the subjects' weight share by the state their earliest interval begins in, or all of it on `initial_state`, and `state_occupation_` is that carried forward by the matrix from zero.

`cumulative_hazard_` runs the increments up and `variance_` runs up the moving weight times the weight staying over the cube of the weight at risk. `confidence_interval_cumulative_hazard_` is the hazard times the exponential of plus and minus a spread, the spread being the normal quantile at half of `alpha` times the root of the variance, all divided by the hazard; it is zero where the hazard is. Transition columns are the two states joined with `->`, the interval columns adding `_lower_` or `_upper_` then one minus `alpha`. `at_risk_` and `transition_counts_` are indexed by the transition times alone; the rest follow `timeline`, each holding what it last was, and a supplied timeline changes no quantity below.

`state_occupation_at(t)` and `predict(times)` report the occupation counting moves at the time asked for, `predict` one row per time. State based outputs align to `states_`, transition based outputs to the transition labels. `expected_length_of_stay(t, from_state=None, since=0.0)` integrates the occupation, which holds from each transition time to the next, between the two times. `expected_number_of_visits(t, from_state=None)` counts the starting state and the expected entries into each state up to `t`. `probability_of_ever_reaching(state, from_state=None)` gives the probability of having reached `state` by each time in `timeline`. `from_state` replaces `initial_distribution_` with all of the weight on that state at time zero, and is carried forward from there like any other starting distribution.

`sojourn_survival(state)` is the Kaplan-Meier estimate of how long one stay lasts, a stay censored unless it ended in an observed move, as a series indexed by the length of the stay. `median_sojourn_time(state)` is where that first reaches a half, or infinity when it never does, and `mean_sojourn_time(state, t)` its area once stays are cut at `t`. `summary` carries `n_transitions` and each transition's final `cumulative_hazard`, in transition order.

With `markov=False` each row instead comes from the subjects occupying that row's state at `since`, which here is the state of the interval starting at or before `since` and ending after it, risk sets and increments rebuilt from those subjects alone. A row nobody occupied is null, a row over a span holding no move the identity.

`lifelines.utils` gains `transitions_from_events(durations, event_observed, initial_state, event_states=None, entry=None)`, each subject leaving `initial_state` once for the state its non zero code names; `transitions_from_state_matrix(df, id_col, censoring_col, initial_state, entry_col=None)`, reading every other column as the time its state was entered, coming under observation at `entry_col` or at zero, closing a subject still observed after its last move with a censored row, following the subjects as given and then time; and `state_matrix_from_transitions(df, initial_state, censoring_col="censoring_time", visit="first")`, the wide form again, one column per state other than `initial_state` holding the first or last time it was entered, which is the stop of a move into it or the start of an interval that simply begins in it, then the last time the subject was seen, subjects and states ascending.

`lifelines.statistics` gains `transition_logrank_test(transitions_A, transitions_B, from_state=None, to_state=None, t_0=-1)` and `multivariate_transition_logrank_test(transitions, groups, from_state=None, to_state=None, t_0=-1)`, comparing how fast groups take one transition by the usual log rank arithmetic on its counts and risk sets, ignoring moves after `t_0` unless negative, testing every transition and naming the rows when no pair is given. A group is a self contained set of subjects, so two groups may reuse an identifier.
