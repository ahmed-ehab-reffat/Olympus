# Add Lagrangian parcel trajectories to the calculation package

Add `parcel_trajectory(u, v, start, step, count, w=None, surface=None, start_time=None)` to `metpy.calc`, along with `sample_trajectory(trajectory, field, method='linear')`, `trajectory_length(trajectory)`, `trajectory_density(trajectory, template, weights=None)`, `trajectory_crossings(trajectory, series, level)`, `trajectory_spread(trajectory)` and `extend_trajectory(trajectory, u, v, count, w=None, surface=None)`, and add `interpolate_to_track(data, longitude, latitude, time=None, vertical=None, method='linear')` to `metpy.interpolate`. Everything after the positional arguments listed here is keyword only, and `step` is either a timedelta or a quantity of time.

Assume parsed MetPy xarray inputs. A parcel is carried over a sphere of radius `earth_avg_radius`, so an eastward wind changes longitude by u divided by the radius times the cosine of the parcel's latitude, and a northward wind changes latitude by v divided by the radius, both converted to degrees. Each step is one classical fourth order Runge-Kutta step over those rates. A negative step runs backward.

Rates come from interpolating each field at a stage's own time and position, linearly over every coordinate it has, in the logarithm of pressure for an isobaric vertical coordinate, and coordinates may increase or decrease. Longitudes are matched to the field's own longitude range modulo 360, and a longitude coordinate whose points plus one spacing span 360 degrees is continuous across the seam. A projected grid is read by converting the position into the grid's coordinates through the field's projection. Nothing is extrapolated: a stage outside any coordinate, or a parcel at a pole, ends the trajectory, and that sample and every later one are missing while earlier ones stand.

The result is a Dataset over `trajectory`, in the order the start points were given, and `step`, running from zero to `count`, with a `time` coordinate along `step` and variables `longitude` in degrees east, `latitude` in degrees north, and, on a layered wind, `vertical` in that coordinate's units. Step zero is the start point and is always reported, even for a parcel that starts outside the field or at a pole, so termination only blanks the samples after it. Every longitude these functions report is wrapped into the interval from -180 up to 180. `start_time` defaults to the field's earliest time going forward and its latest going backward, and is required when the wind has no time coordinate.

A start point is a longitude and latitude, with the level appended when the wind field has a vertical coordinate; one point or a sequence of them is accepted. Such a parcel keeps its level unless `w`, the rate of change of the vertical coordinate in that coordinate's units per second, moves it by the same Runge-Kutta rule, or unless `surface` names a field, such as potential temperature, whose value at the start point the parcel conserves. A conserved parcel is placed afresh at every stage where the surface field takes that value, scanning the levels in the order the coordinate stores them for the first pair that brackets it and interpolating between them the same way the fields are, and it ends where no pair does.

`sample_trajectory` interpolates a field, or every variable of a Dataset, at the samples of a trajectory the same way, giving `trajectory` by `step` in the field's units, missing wherever the parcel is; `method` may be `nearest` to take the closest grid point on every coordinate instead. `interpolate_to_track` does the same for an arbitrary track of positions, flattened if it has more than one dimension, and gives a single `track` dimension. `trajectory_length` is the running great circle distance in metres over the same sphere, zero at step zero, ignoring vertical motion and missing wherever the parcel is.

`trajectory_density` adds the length of each step the parcel completed, scaled by `weights` when given, to the cell of `template` whose grid point is nearest the sample that step starts from, so a parcel contributes its own elapsed time, in seconds on the template's horizontal grid. A step the parcel never finished, one that starts beyond the template, and one without a finite weight all add nothing. `weights` is a quantity along the trajectory, such as a `sample_trajectory` result.

`trajectory_spread` reports the `longitude` and `latitude` of the spherical mean of the samples still running at each step, the mean of their unit vectors put back on the sphere, and their mean great circle distance from it as `spread`, in metres along `step`. `trajectory_crossings` reports the `time`, the `longitude` and `latitude` in the same units the trajectory uses, and the `direction`, 1 upward and -1 downward, of each place where `series` crosses `level`, counting a sample exactly on the level as above it, interpolating linearly between the two samples and the shorter way round when they straddle the seam, and padding the `crossing` dimension where a parcel crosses fewer times. `extend_trajectory` carries a trajectory on from its last sample with the same step, renumbering the steps, and leaves a terminated parcel terminated.

Raise `ValueError` for a count below one, a zero step, wind components that do not share a grid, a start point of the wrong width, a missing `start_time` for a steady field, a vertical velocity or conserved surface without a vertical coordinate, both of those at once, a track that lacks the times or levels its data needs, a track whose positions, times or levels do not all have the same shape, an unknown method, and a field with a vertical coordinate sampled along a trajectory that has none.
