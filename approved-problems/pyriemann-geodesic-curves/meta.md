# Add piecewise-geodesic curves through SPD matrices

Add `pyriemann.geometry.curve`, treating an ordered array of at least two SPD or HPD matrices as the knots of a curve whose consecutive knots are joined by a metric's geodesic. The metric is one of "euclid", "logchol", "logeuclid" and "riemann", listed in `curve_metrics`; any other name raises `ValueError`, as do knots that are not a three-dimensional array of square matrices, or fewer than two. Every routine takes `metric`, defaulting to "riemann", and every parameter-using routine also takes `parameterization`. Every constraint stated here is enforced by raising `ValueError`.

`curve_lengths(C)` returns the geodesic length of each segment. `curve_parameters(C)` returns the parameter of each knot: with `parameterization="arclength"`, the default, the cumulated length up to it over the total, so the first is 0 and the last 1; with "uniform", the knot index over `n_knots - 1`. A curve whose total length is exactly zero has all parameters 0 under "arclength".

`interpolate_curve(C, s)` evaluates the curve at parameters `s`, a scalar or one-dimensional array, clipped into [0, 1], always returning a batch with one matrix per parameter. A parameter inside a segment gives the point of its geodesic at the parameter's share of that range. A parameter equal to a knot's resolves to the lowest-numbered segment ending there, returning that knot. Such a curve returns its first knot everywhere.

`resample_curve(C, n_knots)` evaluates it at `n_knots` equally spaced parameters from 0 to 1, and `resample_curve(C, step=...)` at parameters spaced `step` apart in length from 0, always ending at 1. Step spacing is measured along the curve whatever the parameterization. Exactly one of `n_knots` and `step` is given, `n_knots` at least 2, `step` strictly positive; a zero-length curve cannot be resampled by step.

`project_on_geodesic(X, A, B)` returns for each matrix of `X` the position on the geodesic from `A` to `B` minimizing the distance to it. `X` is one matrix or a set; the result is always a one-dimensional array with one position per matrix. With `clip=True`, the default, positions are restricted to [0, 1]; otherwise the whole extended geodesic is searched. `A` and `B` are square and the same size. `tol` and `maxiter` are accepted search controls. When `A` equals `B` the position is 0.

`project_on_curve(X, C)` returns two arrays: the curve parameter of the point closest to each matrix, and that distance. `X` is again one matrix or a set. The closest point is sought over every segment, not only those beside the closest knot; ties resolve to the lowest-numbered segment. `monotone` is False by default. With `monotone=True` the segments assigned to the matrices are non-decreasing in the order given and minimize the total squared distance, ties resolving to the lowest-numbered segments; each matrix keeps the closest point of its own segment, so the parameters need not be non-decreasing.

`curve_tangent(C, s)` returns the curve velocity at each parameter, as a tangent vector there in `log_map`'s convention: for a step `h` staying inside the same segment, `exp_map` of `h` times the velocity at that point is the point at parameter `s + h`. At a knot it is the velocity of the segment ending there.

`simplify_curve(C, epsilon)` drops knots, always keeping the first and last. Within a part, the interior knot farthest from the geodesic joining its two endpoints is kept when that distance exceeds `epsilon`, and the two halves it creates are treated alike; ties resolve to the lowest-numbered knot. `simplify_curve(C, n_knots=...)` instead keeps adding the farthest knot over all parts, largest distance first, until that many are kept or no interior knot remains. Exactly one of `epsilon` and `n_knots` is given, `epsilon` strictly positive and `n_knots` at least 2. Both forms return the kept knots and their indices in increasing order.

`fit_curve(X, n_segments)` chooses which of the ordered matrices `X` are knots. The first and last are always knots, a segment costs the sum of squared distances from the matrices strictly inside it to that segment, and the chosen knots minimize the total cost over every way of cutting `X` into that many segments; ties resolve to the smallest knot indices. Asking for at least `len(X) - 1` segments keeps every matrix. `fit_curve(X, epsilon=...)` instead uses the fewest segments keeping every matrix within `epsilon` of its segment, and minimizes the total cost among those. Exactly one of `n_segments` and `epsilon` is given, `n_segments` at least 1 and `epsilon` strictly positive. Both forms return the knots and their indices.

`curve_distance(C1, C2)` is the discrete Frechet distance: the smallest over all couplings of the largest distance between coupled knots, a coupling pairing the first knots, then the last, advancing one or both indices at each step. With `return_coupling=True` it also returns, in order, the index pairs of a coupling reaching it, backtracked from the last pair to the cheapest of the three previous, preferring the diagonal step, then the step on the first curve.

`curve_length_between(C, s1, s2)` is the length between two parameters, in any order. `insert_knot(C, s)` inserts the curve point at `s` in place, leaving the curve unchanged as a set of points, and returns them untouched when `s` is already a knot's parameter. `split_curve(C, s)` returns the two curves before and after `s`, strictly inside (0, 1) and belonging to both. `mean_curve(curves)` resamples every curve to `n_knots` knots, the largest knot count among them when not given, and returns the mean of same-rank knots under the metric; at least one curve is needed. Matrices and curves are only combined or compared when they have the same size.

Add `CurveEmbedding` to `pyriemann.embedding`, taking `metric` and `parameterization` too. Without labels it takes the training matrices as knots in order, or fits them with `n_segments` segments; with labels the knots are the mean of each label's matrices, labels in increasing order. Passing both labels and `n_segments` raises `ValueError`. It then simplifies with `epsilon` and resamples to `n_knots`, in that order, when given, storing them in `knots_`. Its `transform` returns each matrix's curve parameter and distance to the curve, and `inverse_transform` returns the curve points at the first column's parameters.
