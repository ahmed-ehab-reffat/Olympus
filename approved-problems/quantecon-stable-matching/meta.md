# Add stable matching markets to QuantEcon

Add a `quantecon.matching` subpackage, imported at the top level beside `quantecon.game_theory`, for matching markets. Every array these routines return holds integers, and every argument described below as a sequence is `array_like`, so a NumPy array works wherever a list does. Preferences are always given as one sequence per agent, listing only the partners that agent finds acceptable, most preferred first. A preference list that holds an index outside the range of the side it refers to, or that holds the same index twice, raises `ValueError`. Two agents on opposite sides are acceptable to each other only when each appears in the other's list.

A two sided matching is a one dimensional integer array of length `m`, the number of proposers, holding the respondent matched to each proposer and -1 where a proposer is unmatched. The optional `caps` gives the number of partners each respondent may take. It holds one strictly positive integer per respondent and defaults to one each; a `caps` of the wrong length, or one holding an entry below one, raises `ValueError`.

`deferred_acceptance(prop_prefs, resp_prefs, caps=None)` returns the proposer optimal stable matching, the stable matching that every proposer likes at least as well as its partner in any other one. `respondent_optimal(prop_prefs, resp_prefs, caps=None)` returns the respondent optimal one in the same proposer indexed form, the stable matching every proposer likes at least as little as any other one.

`blocking_pairs(matching, prop_prefs, resp_prefs, caps=None)` returns the pairs that block the matching, as an array of shape `(k, 2)` sorted lexicographically, of shape `(0, 2)` when there are none. A mutually acceptable pair that is not already matched blocks when the proposer is unmatched or prefers that respondent to its own partner, and the respondent either has a free place or prefers that proposer to its least preferred partner. A partner an agent does not find acceptable counts as no partner at all: it leaves the proposer free to block, it fills no place at the respondent, and it is never the respondent's least preferred partner. This routine and `is_stable` both take a matching of one entry per proposer, each -1 or a respondent index, and both raise `ValueError` on a wrong length or an entry below -1 or past the last respondent.

`is_stable(matching, prop_prefs, resp_prefs, caps=None)` is True when all three of the following hold: every matched pair is acceptable to both of its members, no respondent holds more partners than its capacity, and no pair blocks. Having no blocking pair is on its own not enough.

`stable_matchings(prop_prefs, resp_prefs, caps=None)` returns every stable matching of the market, as an array of shape `(t, m)` whose rows are sorted lexicographically with -1 ordering before 0. Markets with several thousand stable matchings over a few dozen proposers are in scope, so filtering all the ways of pairing the two sides is not a usable route to the answer.

`egalitarian_stable_matching(prop_prefs, resp_prefs)` returns the stable matching with the smallest total rank, counting for every matched pair the position each of its two members gives the other in its own list and nothing for an unmatched agent. `minimum_regret_stable_matching(prop_prefs, resp_prefs)` instead returns the one whose largest single such position is as small as possible. For either, when several stable matchings tie on its measure, the one every proposer likes at least as well as the others is returned, and markets whose stable matchings number in the billions are in scope.

`matching_join(matching1, matching2, prop_prefs)` gives every proposer whichever of its two partners it prefers and `matching_meet(matching1, matching2, prop_prefs)` gives it the one it likes less, being unmatched counting as worse than any acceptable respondent. Each argument must hold one entry per proposer and every entry must be -1 or a respondent that proposer finds acceptable, or `ValueError` is raised.

In a one sided market `prefs` lists the agents that each agent finds acceptable, and an agent may not list itself. A matching there must hold one entry per agent, each of them -1 or an agent index, and it must pair agents symmetrically and match nobody with itself, or `ValueError` is raised. A mutually acceptable pair blocks it when each of the two is unmatched or prefers the other to its current partner, and here too a partner an agent does not find acceptable counts as no partner at all.

`stable_roommate_matchings(prefs)` returns every stable matching of a one sided market, as an array of shape `(t, n)` with rows sorted lexicographically. A one sided market may have none, in which case `t` is zero. `roommate_blocking_pairs(matching, prefs)` returns the blocking pairs as an array of shape `(k, 2)`, of shape `(0, 2)` when there are none, each row holding the smaller of the two indices first and the rows sorted lexicographically. `is_roommate_stable(matching, prefs)` is True when every matched pair is acceptable to both of its members and no pair blocks.

Two allocation mechanisms work with `n` agents and `n` houses numbered alike, `prefs` listing the houses each agent finds acceptable, and both return an array of length `n` holding the house allocated to each agent, or -1 when that agent ends up with nothing.

`top_trading_cycles(prefs, endowment=None)` starts from `endowment`, which must give every agent a distinct house, or `ValueError` is raised, and which defaults to agent `i` owning house `i`. A round first retires every remaining agent that has no acceptable house left, giving it nothing and taking its own house out of the market, repeating that until no more can be retired. Each remaining agent then points at the owner of its most preferred remaining acceptable house, and every agent lying on a cycle of that pointing receives the house it points at and leaves with it. Rounds continue until no agent is left.

`serial_dictatorship(prefs, order)` lets the agents choose one at a time in the given order, each taking its most preferred house among those still free. `order` must list every agent exactly once, or `ValueError` is raised.
