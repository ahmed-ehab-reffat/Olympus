# Add transient waveform simulation for networks and circuits

Add a `skrf.transient` module exporting `Source`, `StepSource`, `PulseSource`, `BitSource`, `ArraySource`, `TransientResult` and `Eye` through `skrf`.

Sources are sampled on the grid by `sample(t)`. `StepSource(amplitude=1, delay=0, rise_time=0)` is zero before `delay`, then ramps linearly to `amplitude` over `rise_time`, instantly if that is zero. `PulseSource(amplitude=1, delay=0, width=0, rise_time=0)` is such a transition at `delay` minus one at `delay + width`. `BitSource(bits, bit_rate, amplitude=1, delay=0, rise_time=0)` holds bit `k` from `delay + k / bit_rate`, uses one transition per change of level, and keeps the last. `ArraySource(v)` gives samples matching the grid length.

`Network.transient(sources, z_source=None, z_load=None, loads=None, window=None)` maps a port index to a source. A driven port carries an ideal source in series with `z_source`, others are terminated by `z_load`; both default to the port reference impedance. All three may be one number or one per port, each real, positive, finite and independent of frequency, though ports may differ. Such a termination is matched and does not reflect. A port in `loads` is instead terminated by a one port network sharing the frequency vector, reflecting any number of times, and may not be driven. `window` is any specification scipy accepts; none is applied by default.

The network is extrapolated to 0 Hz and a uniform step; the record starts at zero, is spaced one over twice the highest frequency and lasts one over the frequency step. Two points are needed and the sweep must be linear. The response is a linear convolution: anything arriving past the record end is dropped, never wrapped to the start. A driven port's incident wave is its source voltage over twice the root of the port impedance, a port voltage is the sum of its waves times that root, and current flows into the port.

`TransientResult` carries `t`, `v`, `i`, `a`, `b`, `dt`, `nports` and `node_voltages`, a mapping from an intersection name to its waveform. `settled_value(port)` is the last sample. `threshold_time(port, level)` interpolates the first crossing of that level linearly, and is not a number when it never happens. `propagation_delay(port, fraction=0.5)` and `rise_time(port, low=0.1, high=0.9)` cross fractions of the settled value, `low` below `high`. `overshoot(port)` is the excursion in volts past it. `impedance_profile(port)` turns the reflection against the settled incident wave of a driven port into ohms, a total reflection being infinite.

`eye(port, bit_rate, delay=0)` folds the record into complete unit intervals from the first sample at or after `delay`, giving an `Eye` with `traces`, one row per interval, `nui`, their count, `offsets`, the time of each column from the interval start, and `threshold`, the mean of the folded samples. The centre column is at half the column count rounded down; traces above the threshold there are the upper ones, `open_columns` are where upper and lower stay apart, `height` is their separation at the centre and `width` their count times the sample spacing. An interval needs two samples and a record two intervals.

`Circuit.transient(sources, window=None)` drives the ports in the order of `port_indexes`, needs a grid starting at 0 Hz, and fills `node_voltages` for every intersection.

Bad input should be rejected rather than quietly turned into a waveform. A sweep that is not linear raises `NotImplementedError`, and that check comes before any other work. `sources` and `loads` may be any mapping, and each source must be a `Source`; anything else is a `TypeError`. The rest raise `ValueError`: the impedance rules above, a negative `rise_time` or `width`, an empty or non binary bit sequence, a bit rate that is not positive, a `sources` that names no port in range, a metric asked for a port that does not exist, and `impedance_profile` on a port that is not driven.
