# Add an arcs line join to the stroke tessellator

lyon's stroke tessellator has no way to keep a stroke's curvature continuous through a corner the way SVG2's arcs join does. Add an `Arcs` variant to `LineJoin` whose join surface still spans the stroke's offset points at the same radius `Round` uses, but bends through an intermediate point positioned along the radius, from the join outward, of the circle passing through the join point and its two neighboring stroke points on the path.

When the join point and its two neighbors are collinear or not all distinct from one another (no such arc exists), or when the join would exceed the same `miter_limit` threshold, the join falls back to a plain `Bevel` corner instead of the curvature-following geometry.

This applies whether the stroke uses a single fixed line width or the line width varies along the path.

Also add three standalone public functions at the crate root, each taking the same three consecutive `Point` centerline points plus a `positive_side: bool` that is `true` for the left side of the direction of travel from the first point through the second to the third, and `false` for the right side. `compute_arcs_join(prev: Point, join: Point, next: Point, half_width: f32, miter_limit: f32, positive_side: bool) -> Option<Point>` and `compute_arcs_join_polyline(prev: Point, join: Point, next: Point, half_width: f32, miter_limit: f32, tolerance: f32, positive_side: bool) -> Vec<Point>` both compute the requested side's join geometry on its own terms, regardless of whether that side happens to be convex or concave for the given points; `compute_arcs_join` returns the single curvature-following point, `None` under the same fallback conditions described above or when the requested side's curvature is too tight relative to `half_width` for such a point to exist, and `compute_arcs_join_polyline` returns the full polyline at the given tolerance, from that side's offset point on the incoming edge through the curvature-following point to its offset point on the outgoing edge, again empty under the same fallback conditions. `arcs_join_side_is_convex(prev: Point, join: Point, next: Point, positive_side: bool) -> bool` reports whether the requested side is the one an actual stroke tessellation draws that curvature-following surface on: the tessellator itself always connects its other, concave side directly, no matter which join is configured, even though the two functions above still compute a result for that side when asked directly.
