# Add aggressive coarsening and multipass interpolation to classical AMG

Add `aggressive_strength_of_connection(S, splitting, degree=2, paths=1)` to `pyamg.classical.split`, taking the off-diagonal sparsity pattern of `S` as the strong-connection graph. The entry for an ordered pair of points that are both coarse in `splitting` counts the distinct fine points lying on a strong path from the first to the second of length at most `degree` whose every intermediate point is fine, each counting once however many such paths use it. Entries below `paths` are dropped. `degree` is at least two and `paths` at least one. The result is square, the size of `S`, with a zero diagonal and nothing in a fine row or column.

Add `aggressive_coarsening(S, degree=2, paths=1, second_pass=False, method='RS')`. It splits `S`, builds the long-range matrix from that splitting and splits again, returning ones and zeros as `intc`. A point fine in the first stage stays fine, and a point coarse in the first stage with no long-range connection either way stays coarse. `method` picks the splitting routine, `RS`, `PMIS` or `CLJP`, and `second_pass` only reaches `RS`. An unknown method is refused.

Add `multipass_interpolation(A, C, splitting, theta=None, norm='min', trunc=None, improve=0, strict=False, lump=False, max_passes=None)` and `interpolation_passes(A, C, splitting, theta=None, norm='min')` to `pyamg.classical.interpolate`. Both work from the entries of `A` that lie in the strength pattern of `C`, or of a pattern recomputed from a `theta` in `[0, 1]` and a `norm`, either `min` or `abs`, as `direct_interpolation` does. Coarse points are settled in pass zero. Each later pass settles every unsettled fine point with a strong neighbour settled earlier: its unscaled row takes the matrix entry for each such coarse neighbour, and for each such fine neighbour that entry times the neighbour's own unscaled row. Passes repeat until one settles nothing. `interpolation_passes` reports, as `intc`, zero for a coarse point, the one-based pass number for a fine point, and minus one for a fine point no pass settles.

Each settled fine row is scaled once, after the passes, by minus the sum of that row's off-diagonal entries of `A` over the product of its diagonal entry and the sum of the unscaled row; a zero diagonal or a zero row sum leaves it unscaled. With `lump` the entries of the strong neighbours a row could not use join that diagonal before it divides. `max_passes` stops the passes early, and a value below one is refused. A coarse row holds a single one, coarse columns numbered by increasing point index, and an unsettled fine point has an empty row unless `strict` asks for an error. `trunc` and `improve` then apply the two routines below, truncation first and the sweeps after it.

Add `truncate_interpolation(P, theta)`, with `theta` in `[0, 1)`, which keeps the entries of each row whose magnitude is at least `theta` times the largest in that row and rescales them so the row sum is what it was, leaving a row alone when the kept entries sum to zero. Add `jacobi_improve_interpolation(A, P, splitting, omega=4.0/3.0, degree=1, C=None)`, which applies `degree` weighted-Jacobi sweeps, replacing `P` by `P` minus `omega` times the inverse diagonal of `A` times `A P`, and restores every coarse row to its single one after each sweep. Given a `C`, the sweep first replaces `A` by its entries inside the pattern of `C`, each row's remaining entries added to its diagonal. `omega` must be positive, and a zero diagonal is an error once a sweep is asked for.

Add `pyamg.classical.sparsify` holding `sparsify_coarse_operator(A, theta=0.1, lump='diagonal', symmetric=True, B=None, protect=None)`, with `theta` in `[0, 1)` and `protect` a CSR matrix shaped like `A`. It keeps the off-diagonal entries of each row whose magnitude is at least `theta` times the largest off-diagonal magnitude in that row, and with `symmetric` also keeps an entry whose transpose is kept. An entry of `A` at a position stored in `protect` is kept whatever the threshold says, and protection creates no entry where `A` has none.

Lumping preserves the image of a vector `B` holding one entry per point, the vector of ones when none is given, so what the row dropped is weighed by `B` before it is put back: onto the diagonal divided by that row's own entry of `B`, or, with `lump` set to `strong`, spread over the kept off-diagonal entries in proportion to their own magnitudes, the share they divide by being those magnitudes each times its column's entry of `B`, falling back to the diagonal when that share is zero. `none` discards the dropped entries. The diagonal is always stored, taking a zero when `A` stored none and nothing was added, and a `B` holding a zero is refused whenever `lump` is anything but `none`, even where nothing is dropped.

`ruge_stuben_solver` gains `aggressive` as a `CF` method, `multipass` as an `interpolation` method, an `aggressive_levels` count limiting aggressive coarsening to that many leading levels with `RS` after it, zero meaning `RS` throughout and no count meaning every level, and a `sparsify` method, `lump`, applied to each Galerkin operator before it joins the hierarchy. `CF` and `sparsify` name a method on its own or pair it with a dictionary of that method's arguments, the way `strength` already does.

Every new name is exported from `pyamg.classical`. Any nonzero mark in a splitting is a coarse point, and `degree`, `paths`, `improve`, `max_passes` and `aggressive_levels` are whole numbers. A complex matrix is carried through, every magnitude being an absolute value. Every matrix argument is a CSR array or a CSR matrix, `A` and `S` are square, a `C` matches the shape of the `A` beside it, a `P` has a row per point and a column per coarse point, and `aggressive_levels` is never negative. Bad values given to the new routines or to the new solver options raise `ValueError`, and a matrix that is not CSR raises `TypeError` in the new routines. Every matrix returned carries sorted indices and stores no zeros, apart from the diagonal a sparsified operator keeps, and none of these routines changes anything it was given.
